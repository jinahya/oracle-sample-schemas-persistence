= HR — identifiers
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3
:sectanchors:

Which column(s) each table and view of the `HR` schema maps as a Jakarta Persistence
`@Id`, and which ones cannot map one at all.

For a table the answer is usually declared: its `PRIMARY KEY` is the identifier, and the
schema guarantees it for any data. For a view — and for the few tables that declare no key
— there is nothing to read off the dictionary, so the answer has to be measured.

Each `src/test/sql/<NAME>.sql` that needs measuring ends in a `CANDIDATE_KEYS` section
holding the two queries the numbers below come from: a single-pass profile counting the
rows, the non-nulls and the distinct values of every column, and a
`GROUP BY ... HAVING COUNT(1) > 1` duplicate check on the candidate the profile suggests.
A column is a single-column candidate when its `NOT_NULLS` and its `DISTINCTS` both equal
`ROW_COUNT`.

Measured against `db-sample-schemas` as installed by `docker-compose-up.sh`. A measured
count describes the shipped data; only a key backed by a constraint, or by a view's own
`GROUP BY`, is guaranteed to hold for any data.

== Tables

Every `HR` table declares a primary key, so none of them needs measuring; the `@Id` is the
key the schema already names.

[cols="3,3,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Table | `PRIMARY KEY` | `UNIQUE` | Mapped by

| `COUNTRIES`   | `COUNTRY_ID`               | —       | `Country`
| `DEPARTMENTS` | `DEPARTMENT_ID`            | —       | `Department`
| `EMPLOYEES`   | `EMPLOYEE_ID`              | `EMAIL` | `Employee`
| `JOBS`        | `JOB_ID`                   | —       | `Job`
| `JOB_HISTORY` | `EMPLOYEE_ID, START_DATE`  | —       | `JobHistoryWithEmbeddedId`, `JobHistoryWithIdClass`
| `LOCATIONS`   | `LOCATION_ID`              | —       | `Location`
| `REGIONS`     | `REGION_ID`                | —       | `Region`
|===

`EMPLOYEES.EMAIL` is `NOT NULL` as well as unique, so it would work as a natural `@Id` —
and is exactly the kind of column an application is expected to let people change, so the
surrogate stays the identifier.

`JOB_HISTORY` is the one composite key, which is why the module maps it twice — once with
an `@EmbeddedId` and once with an `@IdClass`, over the shared `JobHistoryId`. Only one of
the two is listed in `persistence.xml`; see `CLAUDE.md` on `exclude-unlisted-classes`.

== Views

[cols="3,4,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| View | Narrowest unique column(s) | `@Id`? | Why

| `EMP_DETAILS_VIEW`
| `EMPLOYEE_ID`
| yes, single
| `EMPLOYEES`' primary key, joined to at most one row of every other table. Mapped by
  `EmpDetailsView`.
|===

=== EMP_DETAILS_VIEW

106 rows — 107 employees, less the one with no `DEPARTMENT_ID`, which the view's
equi-joins drop.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `EMPLOYEE_ID`     | 106 | 106
| `LAST_NAME`       | 106 | 102
| `FIRST_NAME`      | 106 |  91
| `SALARY`          | 106 |  58
| `JOB_ID`          | 106 |  19
| `JOB_TITLE`       | 106 |  19
| `MANAGER_ID`      | 105 |  18
| `DEPARTMENT_ID`   | 106 |  11
| `DEPARTMENT_NAME` | 106 |  11
| `CITY`            | 106 |   7
| `COMMISSION_PCT`  |  34 |   7
| `LOCATION_ID`     | 106 |   7
| `STATE_PROVINCE`  | 105 |   6
| `COUNTRY_ID`      | 106 |   4
| `COUNTRY_NAME`    | 106 |   4
| `REGION_NAME`     | 106 |   2
|===

`EMPLOYEE_ID` is the `@Id`, and `EmpDetailsView` already maps it as one. Every join in the
view leads from an employee through a foreign key to at most one department, job, location,
country and region, so the view neither multiplies nor merges employee rows: one row per
employee is structural.

`LAST_NAME` comes closest after it and is still not unique — 102 distinct values over 106
rows.

`COMMISSION_PCT` (34 non-nulls) and `MANAGER_ID` / `STATE_PROVINCE` (105) are the nullable
columns; map them as nullable attributes.
