= CO — identifiers
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3
:sectanchors:

Which column(s) each table and view of the `CO` schema maps as a Jakarta Persistence
`@Id`, and which ones cannot map one at all.

For a table the answer is usually declared: its `PRIMARY KEY` is the identifier, and the
schema guarantees it for any data. For a view — and for the few tables that declare no key
— there is nothing to read off the dictionary, so the answer has to be measured.

Each `src/test/sql/<NAME>.sql` that needs measuring ends in a `CANDIDATE_KEYS` section
holding the two queries the numbers below come from: a single-pass profile counting the
rows, the non-nulls and the distinct values of every column, and a
`GROUP BY ... HAVING COUNT(1) > 1` duplicate check on the candidate the profile suggests.
A column is a single-column candidate when its `NOT_NULLS` and its `DISTINCTS` both equal
`ROW_COUNT`.

Measured against `db-sample-schemas` as installed by `_docker-compose-up.sh`. A measured
count describes the shipped data; only a key backed by a constraint, or by a view's own
`GROUP BY`, is guaranteed to hold for any data.

== Tables

Every `CO` table declares a primary key, so none of them needs measuring; the `@Id` is the
key the schema already names.

[cols="3,3,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Table | `PRIMARY KEY` | `UNIQUE` | Mapped by

| `CUSTOMERS`   | `CUSTOMER_ID`             | `EMAIL_ADDRESS`          | `Customer`
| `INVENTORY`   | `INVENTORY_ID`            | `STORE_ID, PRODUCT_ID`   | `Inventory`
| `ORDERS`      | `ORDER_ID`                | —                        | `Order`
| `ORDER_ITEMS` | `ORDER_ID, LINE_ITEM_ID`  | `PRODUCT_ID, ORDER_ID`   | `OrderItemWithEmbeddedId`, `OrderItemWithIdClass`
| `PRODUCTS`    | `PRODUCT_ID`              | —                        | `Product`
| `SHIPMENTS`   | `SHIPMENT_ID`             | —                        | `Shipment`
| `STORES`      | `STORE_ID`                | `STORE_NAME`             | `Store`
|===

Every column named above is `NOT NULL`, so each unique constraint is a usable alternate
key as well — `CUSTOMERS.EMAIL_ADDRESS` and `STORES.STORE_NAME` would each work as a
natural `@Id`. The surrogate is the better identifier all the same: an email address and a
store name are both things an application is expected to change.

`ORDER_ITEMS` is the one composite key, which is why the module maps it twice — once with
an `@EmbeddedId` and once with an `@IdClass`, over the shared `OrderItemId`.

== Views

None of the four views declares anything, so all four were measured.

[cols="3,4,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| View | Narrowest unique column(s) | `@Id`? | Why

| `CUSTOMER_ORDER_PRODUCTS`
| `ORDER_ID`
| yes, single
| the view's `GROUP BY` key, and `ORDERS`' primary key. Mapped by `CustomerOrderProducts`.

| `PRODUCT_ORDERS`
| `PRODUCT_NAME`, `ORDER_STATUS`
| yes, composite
| the view's whole `GROUP BY` key; both columns are `NOT NULL`. Mapped by
  `ProductOrdersWithIdClass` and `ProductOrdersWithEmbeddedId`, over `ProductOrdersId`.

| `PRODUCT_REVIEWS`
| `PRODUCT_NAME`, `REVIEW`
| no
| `REVIEW` is nullable and 4000 characters wide; uniqueness is a coincidence of the data.
  Mapped by `ProductReviews`, which is not an `@Entity`.

| `STORE_ORDERS`
| `STORE_NAME`, `ORDER_STATUS`
| no
| the `GROUPING SETS` key, but both are `NULL` in the subtotal and grand-total rows.
  Mapped by `StoreOrders`, which is not an `@Entity`.
|===

=== CUSTOMER_ORDER_PRODUCTS

1950 rows.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `ORDER_ID`      | 1950 | 1950
| `ORDER_TMS`     | 1950 | 1950
| `ORDER_TOTAL`   | 1950 | 1635
| `ITEMS`         | 1950 | 1313
| `CUSTOMER_ID`   | 1950 |  392
| `EMAIL_ADDRESS` | 1950 |  392
| `FULL_NAME`     | 1950 |  392
| `ORDER_STATUS`  | 1950 |    3
|===

`ORDER_ID` is the `@Id` of `CustomerOrderProducts`. It is what the view aggregates on, and it arrives from
`ORDERS.ORDER_ID`, so one row per order is structural rather than incidental.

`ORDER_TMS` profiles as unique too — 1950 orders that happen to carry 1950 distinct
timestamps — but nothing forbids two orders sharing one. Do not map it as the identifier.

=== PRODUCT_ORDERS

109 rows.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `TOTAL_SALES`  | 109 | 103
| `PRODUCT_NAME` | 109 |  45
| `ORDER_COUNT`  | 109 |  30
| `ORDER_STATUS` | 109 |   3
|===

No single column is unique. `(PRODUCT_NAME, ORDER_STATUS)` is the view's entire `GROUP BY`
list, so it is unique by construction, and both columns are `NOT NULL` — a composite
identifier, mapped both ways: `ProductOrdersWithIdClass` and `ProductOrdersWithEmbeddedId`, over
the shared `ProductOrdersId`. Only the `@EmbeddedId` flavour is listed in `persistence.xml`; see
`CLAUDE.md` on why the two may not both be.

=== PRODUCT_REVIEWS

266 rows — one per review in the `PRODUCTS.PRODUCT_DETAILS` JSON, unnested by `JSON_TABLE`.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `REVIEW`       | 260 | 260
| `PRODUCT_NAME` | 266 |  46
| `AVG_RATING`   | 261 |  31
| `RATING`       | 266 |  10
|===

**No usable `@Id`.** The narrowest unique combination is `(PRODUCT_NAME, REVIEW)` — it has
no duplicate groups — but `REVIEW` is `NULL` in 6 rows and is a `VARCHAR2(4000)`. The JSON
array carries no review id, and nothing stops a product from collecting the same review
text twice, so its uniqueness here is a property of the shipped data, not of the view.

A row of this view is addressable only by its position in the JSON array, which the view does not
expose. `ProductReviews` therefore writes the columns out as a plain class — no `@Entity`, no
`equals`/`hashCode`, every column read-only — rather than claiming an identity it cannot have.

=== STORE_ORDERS

60 rows, from four `GROUPING SETS`: per store and status, per store, per status, and one
grand total.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `TOTAL_SALES`  | 60 | 46
| `ADDRESS`      | 56 | 23
| `STORE_NAME`   | 56 | 23
| `LATITUDE`     | 52 | 22
| `LONGITUDE`    | 52 | 22
| `ORDER_COUNT`  | 60 | 18
| `ORDER_STATUS` | 36 |  3
| `TOTAL`        | 27 |  3
|===

**No usable `@Id`.** `(STORE_NAME, ORDER_STATUS)` is the grouping key and has no duplicate
groups, but `STORE_NAME` is `NULL` in 4 rows and `ORDER_STATUS` in 24 — that is exactly how
`GROUPING SETS` marks a subtotal — and an `@Id` attribute may not be null.

Note that `ALL_TAB_COLUMNS` reports both columns as `NOT NULL`, inherited from the base columns
they are selected from. The data says otherwise, and `StoreOrders` follows the data: it is a plain
class — no `@Entity`, no `equals`/`hashCode` — and maps both columns as nullable.
