= SH — identifiers
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3
:sectanchors:

Which column(s) each table and view of the `SH` schema maps as a Jakarta Persistence
`@Id`, and which ones cannot map one at all.

For a table the answer is usually declared: its `PRIMARY KEY` is the identifier, and the
schema guarantees it for any data. For a view — and for the few tables that declare no key
— there is nothing to read off the dictionary, so the answer has to be measured.

Each `src/test/sql/<NAME>.sql` that needs measuring ends in a `CANDIDATE_KEYS` section
holding the two queries the numbers below come from: a single-pass profile counting the
rows, the non-nulls and the distinct values of every column, and a
`GROUP BY ... HAVING COUNT(1) > 1` duplicate check on the candidate the profile suggests.
A column is a single-column candidate when its `NOT_NULLS` and its `DISTINCTS` both equal
`ROW_COUNT`.

Measured against `db-sample-schemas` as installed by `_docker-compose-up.sh`. A measured
count describes the shipped data; only a key backed by a constraint, or by a view's own
`GROUP BY`, is guaranteed to hold for any data.

IMPORTANT: `sh_create.sql` builds `CAL_MONTH_SALES_MV` and `FWEEK_PSCAT_SALES_MV` before
`sh_populate.sql` loads `SALES`, and never refreshes them, so both materialized views hold
0 rows on a fresh install. Profiling them without a refresh reports every column as unique
and not null, because every count is 0. Refresh first:

[source,sql]
----
EXEC DBMS_MVIEW.REFRESH('SH.CAL_MONTH_SALES_MV', 'C');
EXEC DBMS_MVIEW.REFRESH('SH.FWEEK_PSCAT_SALES_MV', 'C');
----

== Tables

`SH` is the one schema of the three whose tables do not all declare a key: **`COSTS` and `SALES`
declare no primary key and no unique constraint**, and carry only non-unique bitmap indexes on
their dimension columns. Both are measured below; every other table is answered by its constraint.

Where a composite identifier is mapped, it is mapped twice — once with an `@IdClass` and once with
an `@EmbeddedId`, over one shared id class — as `CO.ORDER_ITEMS` and `HR.JOB_HISTORY` already are.
Only the `@EmbeddedId` flavour of each pair is listed in `persistence.xml`; see `CLAUDE.md` for why
the two may not both be.

[cols="4,3,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Table | `PRIMARY KEY` | `UNIQUE` | Mapped by

| `CHANNELS`                   | `CHANNEL_ID` | — | `Channel`
| `COSTS`                      | **none**     | **none** | `CostWithIdClass`, `CostWithEmbeddedId`
| `COUNTRIES`                  | `COUNTRY_ID` | — | `Country`
| `CUSTOMERS`                  | `CUST_ID`    | — | `Customer`
| `PRODUCTS`                   | `PROD_ID`    | — | `Product`
| `PROMOTIONS`                 | `PROMO_ID`   | — | `Promotion`
| `SALES`                      | **none**     | **none** | `SaleWithIdClass`, `SaleWithEmbeddedId`
| `SUPPLEMENTARY_DEMOGRAPHICS` | `CUST_ID`    | — | `SupplementaryDemographics`
| `TIMES`                      | `TIME_ID`    | — | `Time`
|===

`SUPPLEMENTARY_DEMOGRAPHICS.CUST_ID` is both the primary key and a foreign key to
`CUSTOMERS`, so it is a one-to-one extension rather than a table of its own.

=== COSTS

82,112 rows.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `UNIT_COST`  | 82112 | 5675
| `UNIT_PRICE` | 82112 | 3609
| `TIME_ID`    | 82112 | 1459
| `PROD_ID`    | 82112 |   72
| `CHANNEL_ID` | 82112 |    3
| `PROMO_ID`   | 82112 |    3
|===

No single column is anywhere near unique. `(PROD_ID, TIME_ID, PROMO_ID, CHANNEL_ID)` has no
duplicate groups and all four are `NOT NULL` — the grain the table is obviously meant to
have, and the four columns `PROFITS` joins on.

`CostWithIdClass` and `CostWithEmbeddedId` map it both ways over the shared `CostId`, each of the
four columns carrying a read-only `@ManyToOne` to its dimension alongside the `@Id`.

CAUTION: it is a measurement, not a constraint. Nothing in the schema stops a second cost row for
the same product, day, promotion and channel from appearing, and the JPA identity built on those
four columns would silently collapse the two. The mapping is as good as the data; it is not a key
the database will defend.

=== SALES

918,843 rows.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `CUST_ID`       | 918843 | 7059
| `AMOUNT_SOLD`   | 918843 | 3586
| `TIME_ID`       | 918843 | 1459
| `PROD_ID`       | 918843 |   72
| `CHANNEL_ID`    | 918843 |    4
| `PROMO_ID`      | 918843 |    4
| `QUANTITY_SOLD` | 918843 |    1
|===

`(PROD_ID, CUST_ID, TIME_ID, CHANNEL_ID, PROMO_ID)` has no duplicate groups and all five are
`NOT NULL`. `QUANTITY_SOLD` holds a single distinct value across the whole table.

`SaleWithIdClass` and `SaleWithEmbeddedId` map it both ways over the shared `SaleId`.

CAUTION: the same caveat as `COSTS`, and a sharper one: a second sale of the same product to the
same customer on the same day through the same channel under the same promotion is an ordinary
thing for a sales fact table to record, and nothing here forbids it.

== Views

None of the three declares anything, so all three were measured.

[cols="3,4,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| View | Narrowest unique column(s) | `@Id`? | Why

| `PROFITS`
| `CHANNEL_ID`, `CUST_ID`, `PROD_ID`, `PROMO_ID`, `TIME_ID`
| composite, by data only
| `SALES`' grain, carried through the join to `COSTS` — but no base table declares it. Mapped by
  `ProfitsWithIdClass` and `ProfitsWithEmbeddedId`.

| `CAL_MONTH_SALES_MV`
| `CALENDAR_MONTH_DESC`
| yes, single
| the materialized query's `GROUP BY` key. Mapped by `CalMonthSalesMv`.

| `FWEEK_PSCAT_SALES_MV`
| `WEEK_ENDING_DAY`, `PROD_SUBCATEGORY`, `CHANNEL_ID`, `PROMO_ID`
| yes, composite
| the materialized query's whole `GROUP BY` key; all four are `NOT NULL`. Mapped by
  `FweekPscatSalesMvWithIdClass` and `FweekPscatSalesMvWithEmbeddedId`.
|===

=== PROFITS

916,039 rows: the 918,843 rows of `SALES`, less the 2,804 that find no matching `COSTS`
row on `(PROD_ID, TIME_ID, CHANNEL_ID, PROMO_ID)`.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `CUST_ID`       | 916039 | 6119
| `TOTAL_COST`    | 916039 | 5675
| `UNIT_COST`     | 916039 | 5675
| `UNIT_PRICE`    | 916039 | 3609
| `AMOUNT_SOLD`   | 916039 | 3581
| `TIME_ID`       | 916039 | 1459
| `PROD_ID`       | 916039 |   72
| `CHANNEL_ID`    | 916039 |    3
| `PROMO_ID`      | 916039 |    3
| `QUANTITY_SOLD` | 916039 |    1
|===

The five foreign-key columns `(CHANNEL_ID, CUST_ID, PROD_ID, PROMO_ID, TIME_ID)` have no
duplicate groups and are all `NOT NULL` — the grain of `SALES`, preserved because `COSTS`
holds at most one row per `(PROD_ID, TIME_ID, CHANNEL_ID, PROMO_ID)`.

CAUTION: both of those facts inherit the `COSTS` and `SALES` problem above — they are measurements
of the shipped data, not constraints. `ProfitsWithIdClass` and `ProfitsWithEmbeddedId` map it over
`ProfitsId` on that basis, and every column is read-only: it is a view.

=== CAL_MONTH_SALES_MV

48 rows, after a refresh.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Column | Non-nulls | Distincts
| `CALENDAR_MONTH_DESC` | 48 | 48
| `DOLLARS`             | 48 | 48
|===

`CALENDAR_MONTH_DESC` is the `@Id` of `CalMonthSalesMv`: the materialized query groups by it, so one
row per month is structural — the one identifier in this schema that holds without a constraint
behind it.

`DOLLARS` profiles as unique as well — 48 monthly sums that happen not to collide — but it
is the aggregate, not the key.

`(WEEK_ENDING_DAY, PROD_SUBCATEGORY, CHANNEL_ID, PROMO_ID)` is the materialized query's whole
